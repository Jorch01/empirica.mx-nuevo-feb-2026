// IF IT JUST HAPPENED

What to do now.

  1. Don't sign anything else or make new payments.Wait until we review your case.
  2. Gather what you have.Contracts, deeds, receipts and wire transfers, messages and emails, listings, and the details of the seller, the agent or the notary.
  3. Keep everything in writing.Avoid verbal agreements and don't confront the other side.
  4. Don't wait.Each route (civil, criminal or amparo) has its own deadlines, and some are short.
  5. Book the case evaluation.We'll tell you which options you have and what to do first.

Is my case civil or criminal?

It depends on the facts. Fraud can lead to a criminal complaint and, at the same time, a civil lawsuit to recover the property or the money; a breached contract is usually resolved in civil court; and if a government authority affects your property, an amparo may be available. In the case evaluation we tell you which route applies to your case.
